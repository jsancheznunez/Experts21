# Brand positioning & identity

Context: generic "general stores" lose to marketplaces on price (`docs/04-competitors.md`). The brand is our main defense against copycats.

## Positioning statement
```
For [persona] who [need/problem],
[Brand] is the [category/frame] that [key benefit],
unlike [alternative: Temu/Amazon/generic stores], because [reason to believe:
curation, US shipping in 2–7 days, guarantee, expertise, design].
```

## Differentiators that work for dropshipping brands
- **Niche focus:** one audience (e.g. dog owners, small-space living, better sleep).
- **Speed and reliability:** US-warehouse delivery promise backed by data.
- **Curation and quality control:** samples tested, fewer but better products.
- **Bundles and kits** that don't exist on marketplaces.
- **Content and expertise:** how-to videos, guides, community.
- **Guarantee and service:** easy returns, human support.

## Naming checklist
- Short (≤ 2 words / ~12 letters), easy to say and spell, `.com` available or a clean variant.
- Not tied to one product (room to expand within the niche).
- **Clearance:** USPTO trademark search and a web/social handle check before adopting (hand to corporate-IP counsel; see contracts-entity-ip).
- Avoid names that imply medical or unproven claims.

## Identity kit
Voice (3 adjectives + do/don't examples), color palette, typography, logo usage, photo/video style (UGC-native vs studio), packaging/insert ideas (if supplier supports custom branding).

## Output
2–3 positioning options with positioning statements, recommended name candidates (pending clearance), voice guide, and visual direction. Note how each option scores on defensibility vs. copycats.
