**Takeaway:** the global dropshipping market is roughly **$340B–$545B in 2026**, and every major report forecasts **~20–23% annual growth**. The market is large and growing; the hard part is not demand but margin and differentiation.

## Structural shifts that matter in 2026

1. **End of US de minimis.** The $800 duty-free exemption for small parcels ended for China/Hong Kong on **May 2, 2025** (EO 14256) and for all countries on **August 29, 2025** (EO 14324). Every parcel shipped direct from China to a US buyer now pays duties and goes through formal-ish customs processing. De minimis parcel volumes fell about a third after the China change. This raised the landed cost of the classic "AliExpress → US customer" model.
2. **Shift to US/EU-warehoused inventory.** Suppliers (CJdropshipping, Spocket, Doba, TopDawg, etc.) increasingly stock goods in US warehouses: 2–7 day delivery versus 7–30+ days from China. Tariffs are paid once on bulk import rather than per parcel. Vendor anecdotes say US-warehouse options that looked expensive in 2024 are now often the cheaper and faster choice.
3. **Ultra-cheap marketplaces set the price anchor.** Temu, Shein, AliExpress and TikTok Shop sell the same generic goods directly to consumers. A store reselling an undifferentiated Chinese gadget at a 3x markup is easy for shoppers to price-check.
4. **Social commerce growth.** eMarketer forecasts TikTok Shop US sales of about **$23.4B in 2026 (+48% YoY)**. Discovery happens on short-form video; checkout increasingly happens in-app.
5. **Slower overall ecommerce growth.** US ecommerce still grows, but well below pandemic rates (Digital Commerce 360). Growth now comes from taking share.

## What this means for our store

- A **brand-led, niche store with US-warehoused fulfillment** fits 2026 conditions. A generic "general store" shipping from China does not.
- Expect **net margins of 10–20%** after ads, apps, fees, refunds and chargebacks (see `05-startup-and-operating-costs.md`).
- Default market: **United States** (largest ad market, most supplier warehouse coverage), with Canada/UK/Australia as a second wave once fulfillment and tax/VAT are handled.
